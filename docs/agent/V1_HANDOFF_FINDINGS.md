## Release decision and exit scope

**V1.0 release is held.** This ledger is the authoritative open-findings and
exit-status index. Passing local checks or implementing a producer does not
qualify a release. The machine-checked requirements remain owned by
[the public contract](../spec/PUBLIC_CONTRACT_V1.md),
[phase exit requirements](../../config/phase_exit_requirements.toml), and
[the release acceptance matrix](../../config/release_acceptance_matrix.toml).
Every outstanding correctness, security, resource, performance, packaging and
portability finding below is part of the V1.0 exit scope. No finding may be
accepted as debt, waived or deferred past release qualification. Close a finding only
after its complete consumer family is integrated and its stated acceptance
evidence passes; retain its stable ID, integrated repair and explicit unverified
coordinates. Consolidation must preserve every requirement and machine-consumed ID.

This closure standard applies retrospectively to every earlier finding,
including the historical fixed rows below. A landed repair does not establish
closure of its bug family. Closure requires a failure-mechanism search through
sibling implementations, producers and consumers; one corrected authority;
independent regression controls that exercise the actual boundary; and explicit
coverage of affected target and platform cells. Recurring integration or proof
failures must be traced to their shared cause as well. Reconcile uncovered
instances and missing qualification into the existing owning open requirement;
retain the historical repair evidence without treating it as release acceptance.

Every change must distinguish development/compiler work from execution in the
shipped end-user binary. Source custody, proof instrumentation and release
evidence belong on the development side. Additional runtime work must implement
a concrete runtime requirement and justify its measured cost; do not charge
development validation to guest startup, allocations or execution.

CPython source at the declared reference version is the semantic source of
truth. Source-grounded implementation and version-matched independent execution
must agree before a Python semantic finding can close; a fixture or local test
result alone cannot redefine the contract.
